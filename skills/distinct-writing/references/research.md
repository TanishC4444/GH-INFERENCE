# Research basis

Reviewed 19 October 2026. This is an editorial skill, not an AI detector or a
validated intervention. No source establishes a universal frequency ranking of
AI-writing signs across all models, languages, and genres. The compact checklist
combines research-supported tendencies with explicitly identified craft judgments.

## Empirical findings

- [Reinhart et al., *Do LLMs write like humans?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16107)
  compare matched human and model writing using grammatical and rhetorical
  features. Vocabulary and syntax differ by model and register; instruction-tuned
  variants often diverge more than base models. Larger models do not automatically
  resolve these stylistic differences. The study supports genre-sensitive editing,
  not a universal forbidden-word list. Findings from its model sample cannot certify
  the behavior of our current four writers.
- [Kobak et al., *Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications
  through excess vocabulary*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12219543/)
  document population-level shifts in scientific vocabulary associated with LLM
  adoption. This is evidence about a particular domain and time period, not proof
  that an individual text containing a word was generated. Academic vocabulary
  findings should not be transferred directly to screenplay dialogue.
- [Doshi and Hauser, creative-writing experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506)
  find that AI-supplied story ideas can improve individual evaluations while making
  stories more similar to each other. This concerns AI-assisted humans and the
  study's experimental conditions; it does not show that all AI stories are poor
  or that every form of assistance reduces diversity. It motivates attention to
  distinctive premises and character decisions.

## Practical pattern inventories

- [EQ-Bench Slop Score](https://eqbench.com/slop-score.html) compares overrepresented
  words, three-word phrases, and contrast constructions. Its developer explicitly
  distinguishes it from an AI detector and recommends multiple samples because
  subject matter can skew a single text. It is a useful source of inspection
  targets, not a measure of literary merit. Avoid optimizing prose solely to lower
  its score.
- [Wikipedia's community guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
  records inflated significance, superficial analysis, repetitive rhetorical
  structures, and formatting habits. These are editorial observations from an
  encyclopedia context, not controlled evidence of prevalence in fiction. The
  skill uses the general warning against empty rhetoric; it does not import
  Wikipedia's formatting preferences into every genre.

## Reflection on our samples

The local [four-model report](../../../docs/reconciliation/desktop-evidence/style-report.txt) contains one unedited
scene per model under a shared airlock prompt. All four introduce red emergency
lighting, hissing air, and a character with crossed arms; several use a hovering
hand or softening gaze. This is a small, prompt-confounded observation, not a
prevalence estimate: those details can be appropriate in an airlock scene.
The useful revision question is whether the detail has a specific dramatic
function or merely fills a familiar slot.

Recommendations about playable actions, emotional subtext, meaningful changes per
exchange, and earned resolutions are craft judgments informed by those samples.
They have not been experimentally established here as AI-specific traits. The
rule against indiscriminate smoothing protects intentionally blunt, ornate,
regional, fragmentary, or repetitive voices from being flattened into one style.

## Evaluation later

Compare outputs with and without the skill using identical prompts and matched
settings across several genres and seeds. Blind the model/condition labels; judge
specificity, voice distinction, coherence, repetition, and compliance separately.
Inspect whether the skill creates its own repeated cadence. Lower phrase counts
alone do not demonstrate improvement. Do not claim detector evasion or guaranteed
human-like output.
